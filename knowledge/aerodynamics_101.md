# Aerodynamics 101: Key Concepts for Airfoil Design

## 1. Introduction to Airfoils
An airfoil is the cross-sectional shape of a wing, blade (of a propeller, rotor, or turbine), or sail. An airfoil-shaped body moved through a fluid produces an aerodynamic force. The component of this force perpendicular to the direction of motion is called lift. The component parallel to the direction of motion is called drag.

## 2. Geometric Parameters
- **Chord Line**: The straight line connecting the leading and trailing edges.
- **Chord Length (c)**: The length of the chord line.
- **Mean Camber Line**: The locus of points halfway between the upper and lower surfaces.
- **Camber**: The distance between the mean camber line and the chord line, usually measured perpendicular to the chord line. Maximum camber is a key parameter.
- **Thickness**: The distance between the upper and lower surfaces, measured perpendicular to the camber line.

## 3. NACA 4-Digit Series
The NACA 4-digit airfoils (e.g., NACA 2412) follow a specific naming convention:
- **1st Digit**: Maximum camber as percentage of the chord (e.g., '2' -> 2% camber).
- **2nd Digit**: Distance of maximum camber from the leading edge in tenths of chord (e.g., '4' -> 40% chord).
- **3rd & 4th Digits**: Maximum thickness as percentage of the chord (e.g., '12' -> 12% thickness).

**Example: NACA 2412**
- Max Camber: 2%
- Pos of Max Camber: 40%
- Max Thickness: 12%

## 4. Lift and Drag
- **Lift Coefficient ($C_l$)**: A dimensionless coefficient that relates the lift generated by a lifting body to the fluid density around the body, the fluid velocity, and an associated reference area.
  $$ L = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 S C_l $$
- **Drag Coefficient ($C_d$)**: A dimensionless quantity that is used to quantify the drag or resistance of an object in a fluid environment such as air or water.
  $$ D = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 S C_d $$
- **Lift-to-Drag Ratio (L/D)**: The amount of lift generated by a wing or vehicle, divided by the aerodynamic drag it creates by moving through air. L/D is an Efficiency number.

## 5. Reynolds Number (Re)
The Reynolds number is a dimensionless quantity that helps predict flow patterns in different fluid flow situations.
$$ Re = \frac{\rho v L}{\mu} = \frac{v L}{\nu} $$
- **Significance**: Low Re usually implies laminar flow, which is smooth but unstable. High Re implies turbulent flow. Airfoils behave very differently (especially stall characteristics) at different Reynolds numbers.

## 6. Stall
A stall is a reduction in the lift coefficient of an airfoil as the angle of attack increases. This occurs when the critical angle of attack of the airfoil is exceeded. The boundary layer separates from the upper surface, creating a turbulent wake and destroying lift.
